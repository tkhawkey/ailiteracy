The Mantis Shrimp

The mantis shrimp is one of the most extraordinary creatures on Earth — and one of the least famous. It lives in shallow tropical waters, hiding in burrows and crevices on the seafloor, and it rarely grows longer than 30 centimetres. For something so small, it is almost unbelievably powerful.

The mantis shrimp's most famous feature is its punch. It strikes prey using two club-like appendages that accelerate faster than a bullet fired from a gun. The impact is powerful enough to shatter glass, and the movement happens so quickly — in around 1/1000th of a second — that it creates a flash of light and a shockwave in the water. This shockwave can stun or kill nearby prey even if the punch itself misses.

But the punch is not even the strangest thing about it. The mantis shrimp has 16 types of colour receptor in its eyes, compared to just 3 in human eyes. You might expect this to mean it sees the world in extraordinary colour — but scientists discovered that it actually processes colour very differently from us. Rather than blending colours together the way humans do, it identifies them almost like a barcode scanner, making incredibly fast decisions about what it is looking at. Its eyes also move independently from each other, meaning each eye can look in a completely different direction at the same time.

The mantis shrimp can also see ultraviolet light and polarised light — types of light that are completely invisible to humans. Scientists believe it uses polarised light to communicate secretly with other mantis shrimps, in a kind of private visual language that predators cannot intercept.

Despite being such a beautiful and interesting creature, the mantis shrimp is rarely kept in aquariums.
