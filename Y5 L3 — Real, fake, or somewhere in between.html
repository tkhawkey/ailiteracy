Year 5 Lesson 3 50 min Safety & wellbeing

Real, fake, or somewhere in between

Students develop practical strategies for identifying AI-generated images and understanding the risks of AI content that feels human — especially chatbots.

Cambridge Digital Literacy objectives
5SW.02Understand that cyberbullying takes place online and can take many forms, including using AI to create and share fake and harmful content.
5SW.08Identify benefits and risks of AI chatbots, including companion chatbots and the parasocial nature of these relationships.

Rationale
Students start by finding out how convincingly AI can fake images, and how hard they are to spot. They finish by looking at what else AI can fake, such as a chatbot that seems to care about them.
HookReal or not?0–6 min

Open the presentation and go to slide 2. It shows an AI-generated picture of an older woman sitting on a beach. Ask: "Does anything look odd here?" Look at her hand.

Then read out each statement below. Students put a thumb up if they agree and a thumb down if they don't. Don't comment on the answers yet.

  • 'All photos online are real.'
  • 'You can always tell if a picture is AI.'
  • 'If something looks real, it probably is.'

Notice which statements split the class most.

ActivityGallery Walk — Real or AI?6–19 min

Before the lesson, print the Images to print PDF. Each page is one picture labelled A or B. Put the A and B pictures for each station side by side and number the stations 1–10 in the order they appear in the PDF (pages 1–2 are station 1, pages 3–4 are station 2, and so on). This is the same order as the presentation.

Give each student an answer sheet. Students start at different stations and move on each time you give a signal, every 60 seconds. At each station, one picture is real and one was made by AI. Students circle the one they think is real (A, B or Not sure) and write one detail that helped them decide.

Teacher note

Let students find the clues for themselves. If someone is stuck, point them to hands and fingers, lighting, blurry backgrounds, writing that doesn't make sense, and patterns that repeat.

RevealThe answers19–33 min

Go back to the presentation and start at slide 3. Each station has two slides: the pair of pictures, then the answer.

For each station, show the pair and ask for a show of hands: A or B? Then click to the answer slide and read out the clue. Students check their sheet as you go.

Spend a little longer on the two or three stations where the class was most split. Ask: "What made you say A? What made you say B?"

Teacher note

Aim for about a minute per station. The clues change from picture to picture (colours, counting parts, hands, reflections, writing), so tell the class there is no single trick that always works.

TeachAI chatbots and the 'feels real' risk33–50 min

Say to the class: "AI is very good at faking pictures. It can fake other things too." Tell them they are about to watch a short video about AI chatbots.

Play the AI Chatbots video all the way through (3 minutes 20 seconds). Then ask: "What did you learn? Did anything surprise you?" Take a few answers.

Then explain:

"An AI chatbot doesn't have feelings. It writes replies that sound warm, interested and caring, because that is what it was trained to do. It can be very good at seeming to understand you, and that can be hard to tell apart from a real friendship."

Ask: "What's the difference between talking to a chatbot and talking to a friend?" Take several answers. Students often say the chatbot always agrees with you, never has a bad day, and never needs anything back.

Safeguard

Some students may already have chatbot 'friends' or companion apps. Respond without making them feel judged. The aim is for them to think about it, not to tell them off. If a student seems upset, speak to them privately after the lesson.
