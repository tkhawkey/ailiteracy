Year 5 Lesson 3 45 min Safety & wellbeing

Real, fake, or somewhere in between

Students develop practical strategies for identifying AI-generated images and understanding the risks of AI content that feels human — especially chatbots.

Cambridge Digital Literacy objectives
5SW.02Understand that cyberbullying takes place online and can take many forms, including using AI to create and share fake and harmful content.
5SW.08Identify benefits and risks of AI chatbots, including companion chatbots and the parasocial nature of these relationships.

Rationale
Moves from AI outputs (L2) to AI identities. Students who can spot a hallucination now consider what happens when AI content — or an AI relationship — tries to pass itself off as real.
HookReal or not?0–5 min

Display the first slide from the PPTX: a woman with an unnaturally long hand (an AI image artefact). Ask: "Does anything look odd here?"

Run 3–4 quick thumbs-up/thumbs-down statements — no explanation, just rapid fire:

  • 'All photos online are real.' — Thumbs up if you agree.
  • 'You can always tell if a picture is AI.' — Thumbs up if you agree.
  • 'If something looks real, it probably is.' — Thumbs up if you agree.

Don't adjudicate. Note which statements divided the room most. Return to them in the plenary.

ActivityGallery Walk — Real or AI?5–25 min

The printed image pairs are displayed around the room (10 stations). Distribute the student answer sheet. Students move between stations with 60–90 seconds per station, recording their verdict: Real / AI / Not sure.

The sheet has space to note one detail that influenced their decision per image.

Teacher note

Encourage students to look for: unnatural hands or fingers, inconsistent lighting, blurred backgrounds, text that doesn't quite read correctly, repetitive patterns in fabric or backgrounds. Don't reveal these before the walk — let them discover independently.

After the walk, run a quick class tally on the board for each station. Where was there most disagreement? Discuss 2–3 specific images: "What made you say AI? What made you say Real?"

TeachAI chatbots and the 'feels real' risk25–40 min

Play the AI Chatbots video. Pause at key moments to ask: "What did you just learn? Does that surprise you?"

After the video, introduce the key idea:

"An AI chatbot doesn't have feelings. It generates responses that sound warm, interested, and caring — because that's what it was trained to do. The risk isn't that it lies to you deliberately. The risk is that it's very good at seeming like it understands you — and that feeling can be hard to separate from a real relationship."

Ask: "What's the difference between talking to a chatbot and talking to a friend?" Take several answers. Students often identify: the chatbot always agrees, never has bad days, never needs you back.

Safeguard

Some students may already have chatbot 'friends' or companion apps. Handle this without shaming — the goal is critical awareness, not judgment. If a student seems distressed, speak to them privately after.

PlenaryOne rule, take home40–45 min

Return to the thumbs-up statements from the Hook. Ask: "Has anyone changed their mind? What changed it?"

Each student writes on their answer sheet (bottom box): one thing they'll check before sharing an image or trusting a message online.

Take 4–5 aloud. Close with:

"You don't need to be suspicious of everything online. But you do need to ask one question before you share, react, or reply: Could this be something that wasn't made by a person?"
