Year 5 Lesson 1 45 min Prompting & tools

Ask It Better

Students understand that prompt quality determines output quality, and leave with a practical three-part framework — Role, Task, Detail — they can apply immediately.

Cambridge Digital Literacy objectives
5TC.06 Explore how prompting frameworks can be used when interacting with AI tools.
5TC.08 Explore how refining initial text and verbal prompts can impact the accuracy and relevance of GenAI results.
Resources needed
  • Teacher laptop + projector
  • Copilot (or school AI tool) — 2 windows side-by-side, set up before class
  • No student devices required
  • Y5 L1 — Student resource to print.pdf — one per pair
Rationale
Students have been aware of AI since Year 4 but have never been taught to communicate with it deliberately. This lesson surfaces that gap in the first minute — without any technology — then fills it with a practical framework that underpins every lesson in the suite from here on.
Hook Plan me a party 0 – 5 min

Start by saying this — no build-up, no explanation:

"Plan me a party."

Wait. Students will hopefully fire back clarifying questions: How many people? What's the budget? Where is it? Write them on the board as fast as they come. Do not answer them.

Once the board is full, explain: "Every question you just asked represents information that was missing from my instruction. AI has exactly the same problem when we talk to it vaguely."

Teach The three ingredients 5 – 12 min

Write these three words on the board. Keep them visible for the rest of the lesson.

Role
Who should the AI pretend to be?
Task
What exact action do you want it to take?
Detail
What specific constraints or facts must it know?
Teacher note

Frame these as questions to ask yourself before typing — not a rigid rulebook to memorise. The goal is a habit, not a formula. Keep pointing back to the three words on the board when they come up during the lesson.

Live AI Demo Bad vs. good — side-by-side comparison 12 – 22 min

Open Copilot in two side-by-side windows on the projector. Paste these prompts into them .

Bad prompt — paste first
"Tell me about animals."
Expected: dense, dry, textbook-style. Ask: "Would you actually sit and read this? Is it useful to you right now?"
Good prompt — paste second
"You are a nature expert explaining things to a 10-year-old. Give me five surprising facts about deep sea creatures that most people don't know. Write each fact in one or two sentences."
Expected: engaging, crisp, age-appropriate. Ask: "What specific changes made the difference?"
Setup safeguard

Log in and arrange both windows before students arrive. If Copilot adds automated conversational follow-ups, point it out: "The AI is guessing what you want next — the choice to click always belongs to you."

Activity Pair prompt engineering 22 – 35 min

Hand out Y5 L1 — Student resource to print. Each pair selects one of the five prompts and rewrites it using the Role / Task / Detail framework. The prompts are printed without hints — pairs must identify what is missing themselves.

Teacher reference — what each prompt is missing (do not share with students):

# Prompt What's missing
1 "Help me with my homework." Subject, specific question, age, target format, length
2 "Tell me something interesting." Domain/topic, target age, focus area, format
3 "Make me a quiz." Topic, audience, number of questions, style, answer key inclusion
4 "What should I do for my history project." Topic focus, scope, age, prior knowledge — high risk of outsourcing core thinking
5 "Tell me about space." Specific celestial body or phenomenon, depth, layout
Teacher note — Prompt 4

Watch whether pairs ask the AI for answers or for good investigation questions. If they ask for answers, prompt them: "But then whose thinking is it?"

Live AI Demo Student prompts — closing the loop 35 – 42 min

Select 2–3 standout student-written prompts and run them live in Copilot via the projector. Evaluate each output together:

"Did our ingredients work? Did the AI give exactly what we designed for? Is there still anything missing?"

Keep this tight. The goal is to show the direct connection between precise input and quality output — not to test every pair's prompt.

Safety net

If a student prompt produces an odd or messy result, frame it as a win: "Look — the AI still got confused. What specific detail did we forget to protect against?"

Plenary Landing the idea 42 – 45 min
"Precision is a superpower. A vague question gets a vague answer — whether you're asking a person, a search engine, or an AI. The people who master tools are always the ones who learn to be exceptionally clear about what they want."

Ask: "Where else in life does being precise with your words matter?" Take 2–3 responses.

Links forward

Role / Task / Detail reappears in Y6 L3 (Sharpen it) and is formalised as full prompt engineering in Y8 L3 (Engineer the prompt).

Keep exploring

Consider using future tutor time to practise this skill further - little and often! Or, you might share examples from your own AI use where you either got a poor result from not being specific enough, or a good result form being specific.

Key idea

The quality of what you get from AI depends entirely on the quality of what you ask — and asking well is a learnable skill.
