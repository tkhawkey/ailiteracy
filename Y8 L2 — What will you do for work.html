Year 8 Lesson 2 50 min AI & the world

What will you do for work?

Students move from fear ('AI will take all the jobs') to agency ('what do I develop that AI makes more valuable?') — and make the argument themselves through Dragon's Den.

Cambridge Digital Literacy objectives
5DW.03Explore a range of perspectives related to the transformation of jobs that result from AI developments.
7DW.01Make predictions about future technologies, including Artificial General Intelligence, and how these might impact individuals and society.
7DW.10Compare and contrast the capabilities of AI with human intelligence in various contexts such as problem solving, creativity and emotional understanding.
9DW.02Predict long-term societal and economic implications of AI, including the reimagining of job roles and the related skill requirements.

Rationale
These students are 2–3 years from subject choices that shape careers. This lesson builds the habit of thinking clearly about AI and employment — not predicting the future, but reasoning about it well.
Hook10 jobs — show of hands0–5 min

Project or read aloud 10 jobs: data entry clerk, radiologist, factory worker, primary school teacher, junior lawyer, copywriter, social worker, graphic designer, plumber, journalist.

Quick show of hands for each: "Still here in 2040?" Don't resolve disagreement — the point is to surface that students have strong intuitions but uncertain reasoning.

"Let's spend this lesson figuring out what you actually think — and why."
ActivityJob Cards sort5–18 min

Groups of 3–4 receive a set of Job Cards (20 jobs). Sort into three columns: Replaced by AI / Augmented by AI / Still fully human.

No right answers — the discussion is the task. After 10 minutes, each group nominates their most surprising card and shares it with the class.

Teacher notes patterns on the board without resolving them: "I'm hearing 'empathy', 'trust', 'physical presence' coming up a lot. Let's explore that."

ActivityHuman Advantage poster18–32 min

Assign each group a profession (doctor, teacher, architect, journalist, social worker). Scenario: AI becomes extraordinarily capable in the next 10 years. Complete the two-column Human Advantage poster:

  • AI advantage — tasks AI can do better, faster, cheaper.
  • Human advantage — tasks requiring human presence, judgement, relationship.

Plus a third question: If AI does the first column, what does the human's job actually become?

Groups share (1 min each). Teacher draws out the pattern across professions: empathy, accountability, physical presence, trust, creativity with real stakes — without naming it too early.

ActivityDragon's Den32–44 min

Three volunteers take Den seats at the front. Each receives a role card: Nurse, Teacher, Journalist (or similar). Their job: pitch to the Den (the class) why their role still needs a human in 2040.

60 seconds to prepare. 90 seconds to pitch. After each pitch, the class can challenge (raise hand, teacher moderates).

After all three pitches, ask: "Which pitch was most convincing — and what made it work?" Usually the answer involves specific, concrete examples rather than general claims.

Teacher note

The goal is quality of reasoning, not confident conclusions. Reward students who identify genuine tensions — 'AI could do X, but humans need to do Y because...' — over those who simply assert 'humans are better'.

PlenaryExit reflection strip44–50 min

Students complete the exit reflection strip: "The most valuable skill I should develop is _______ because in a world with AI, _______ matters more, not less."

Take 4–5 responses aloud. Teacher names what kept coming up: judgement, empathy, creativity, accountability.

"We didn't predict the future today. We built a way of thinking about it. That's the skill — not knowing what's coming, but being able to reason clearly when it arrives."

Key idea

The question isn’t which jobs AI will replace — it’s which human qualities will become more valuable, not less, as AI keeps improving.
