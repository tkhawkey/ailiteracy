Year 6 · Lesson 2 · Role cards
Who made this — and who owns it?

Read your card carefully. Agree on your two strongest arguments. Choose two speakers. The rest of your group join the jury.

Role Card · Year 6 · AI & the world
The Artist
You've spent years developing your unique style. An AI company trained their model on your work — without asking.

You care most about

Control over your own creative work.

Your main arguments

  • Your style took years of hard work — it belongs to you.
  • Nobody asked your permission and you weren't paid.
  • People who used to hire you are now using AI instead.
  • There is no way to opt out — your work is used whether you like it or not.
  • AI-generated images 'in your style' are sold commercially, often undercutting your prices.

Other angles worth having ready

  • Should AI-generated work be labelled so buyers know what they're getting?
  • What happens to the next generation of artists if there's no career to aim for?
"You shouldn't be able to profit from someone's work without asking."
Role Card · Year 6 · AI & the world
The AI Company
You built a tool used by millions. You trained it on publicly available images — now an artist is taking you to court.

You care most about

Innovation and making creativity available to everyone.

Your main arguments

  • AI learns patterns across millions of works — it doesn't reproduce any single piece.
  • Humans learn from existing art; that's how creativity has always worked.
  • Your tool lets people who couldn't afford an artist create things they're proud of.
  • Restricting training data would slow all AI development.
  • Many artists actually use AI tools themselves to work faster and more freely.

Other angles worth having ready

  • Search engines index the whole internet — is that stealing too?
  • Some artists have benefited from AI tools finding and promoting their work to new audiences.
"Learning is not the same as stealing."
Role Card · Year 6 · AI & the world
The Everyday User
You're a small business owner. AI lets you create professional visuals on a tiny budget. The court case might shut it all down.

You care most about

Access to tools that level the playing field.

Your main arguments

  • You genuinely couldn't afford to commission a professional artist.
  • Technology has always changed industries — cameras didn't end painting, they changed it.
  • You're transparent about using AI; you're not claiming you made it yourself.
  • Small businesses and charities benefit enormously from affordable creative tools.
  • Banning AI tools would widen the gap between rich and poor organisations.

Other angles worth having ready

  • You'd happily pay a small licensing fee if artists were compensated fairly.
  • The problem isn't the tool — it's the lack of rules around it.
"Creativity shouldn't only belong to people who can afford it."
Role Card · Year 6 · AI & the world
The Young Creator
You're 13. You've grown up with these tools. You use AI to experiment — but you're genuinely not sure where your work ends and the AI's begins.

You care most about

What creativity looks like for your generation.

Your main arguments

  • Every artist learns from what came before — you're doing the same thing, just faster.
  • You always add your own thinking, editing, and decisions on top of what AI produces.
  • You're not selling anything — you're learning and experimenting.
  • Growing up with AI tools doesn't make you lazy — it makes you fluent in a new language.
  • The question 'is it really yours?' applies to humans too — nobody creates in a vacuum.

Other angles worth having ready

  • You feel genuinely conflicted — you don't think artists are wrong, but you're not doing something terrible.
  • Is using a calculator cheating at maths? Where does a tool end and cheating begin?
"The future should work for both human and AI creativity — not force us to choose."
Role Card · Year 6 · AI & the world
The Jury
Your job isn't to cheer for a side. It's to listen carefully and reach a verdict together.

You care most about

A fair, reasoned conclusion.

Your main arguments

  • Who benefits — and who gets harmed?
  • Is learning from art the same as copying it?
  • Should permission matter even if something is publicly visible?
  • What would a fair rule actually look like?
  • Does anyone's argument change your mind — and why?

Other angles worth having ready

  • A | AI companies should be free to train on publicly available work — no restrictions.
  • B | AI companies can train, but artists must be credited or paid a share of profits.
  • C | AI companies must ask permission before using anyone's work — opt-in only.
  • D | Something else — design your own solution and defend it.
"We reached our verdict because..."
