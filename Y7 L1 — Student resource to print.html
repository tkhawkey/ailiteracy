Part 2: Bubble Scenario Cards

Your group will receive one scenario. Read it, discuss the three questions, and be ready to share your 'missing content' finding with the class.

Scenario A Lena, 14

Six months ago, Lena started watching videos about competitive swimming after joining a club. She now trains hard and follows elite athletes online.

Her feed is now almost entirely swimming content: technique videos, race highlights, nutrition for athletes, and commentary about competitions she doesn't even follow yet.

Discuss with your group

1. What does this person's feed probably look like now?

2. What are they not seeing anymore?

3. Is that a problem, and if so, for whom?

Scenario B Marcus, 15

Marcus got into political debate content after watching one video arguing that young people don't care about democracy. He found it interesting and kept watching.

His feed is now saturated with opinion content, all broadly from the same political perspective. Commentary on the 'other side' is rare, and always framed negatively.

Discuss with your group

1. What does this person's feed probably look like now?

2. What are they not seeing anymore?

3. Is that a problem, and if so, for whom?

Scenario C Aisha, 13

Aisha's family moved schools last year. She started watching videos about anxiety and feeling like an outsider. It felt comforting to know others felt the same.

Her feed now surfaces almost exclusively mental health content, some helpful, some less so. Upbeat or social content barely appears. The algorithm has decided what 'type' she is.

Discuss with your group

1. What does this person's feed probably look like now?

2. What are they not seeing anymore?

3. Is that a problem, and if so, for whom?

Scenario D Tom, 16

Tom loves gaming and started following his favourite streamer's community two years ago. He now watches 3–4 hours of gaming content a day.

His feeds across YouTube, TikTok, and Discord are nearly identical: gaming reviews, streamer drama, and gaming news. News, science, or anything 'outside' the space rarely appears.

Discuss with your group

1. What does this person's feed probably look like now?

2. What are they not seeing anymore?

3. Is that a problem, and if so, for whom?

Scenario E Priya, 14

Priya is passionate about climate. Six months ago she started following several climate activists and sharing their posts regularly.

Her social media feed now shows almost nothing but climate content, mostly alarming, rarely solutions-focused. Moderate or counter-perspectives have all but disappeared.

Discuss with your group

1. What does this person's feed probably look like now?

2. What are they not seeing anymore?

3. Is that a problem, and if so, for whom?

Scenario F Alex, 13

Alex clicked on a conspiracy video by accident and was briefly fascinated. They watched three more out of curiosity.

Despite not actively seeking it out again, Alex's recommendations now regularly surface conspiracy-adjacent content, 'what they don't want you to know' style videos across multiple platforms.

Discuss with your group

1. What does this person's feed probably look like now?

2. What are they not seeing anymore?

3. Is that a problem, and if so, for whom?
